Our picks, in detail

Best overall

Beelink EQ12 (Intel N100)

The default answer. The N100 sips power at idle, handles a dozen Docker containers without breaking a sweat, and the EQ12's dual 2.5GbE ports make it a natural fit for router or NAS duty. If you buy one box and stop thinking about it, buy this one.

  • CPU: Intel N100, 4C/4T, 6W TDP
  • RAM: 16GB DDR5 (typical config)
  • Storage: 512GB NVMe, expandable
  • Net: 2× 2.5GbE
  • Idle draw: widely reported ~7–10W at the wall*
  • Price: ~$199

Good

  • Excellent performance per watt
  • Dual 2.5GbE — rare at this price
  • Silent under server loads

Watch out

  • Single-channel RAM on some configs
  • Weak for 4K Plex transcodes

*Illustrative range based on reviewer reports, not our own measurement.

Best budget

GMKtec NucBox G3 (Intel N100)

Same N100 chip as the EQ12, usually $30–50 cheaper. You give up DDR5 (it's DDR4 here) and some chassis polish, but for Pi-hole, Uptime Kuma, and a handful of containers, it's all the computer you need.

  • CPU: Intel N100, 4C/4T, 6W TDP
  • RAM: 8GB DDR4 (typical config)
  • Storage: 512GB NVMe
  • Net: 2× 2.5GbE
  • Price: ~$159

Good

  • Lowest entry price for a real N100 box
  • Still gets dual 2.5GbE

Watch out

  • 8GB RAM limits heavier stacks
  • DDR4, not DDR5
Newest chip

Beelink EQ14 (Intel N150)

The N150 is Intel's Twin Lake refresh of the N100 — same 6W envelope, slightly higher clocks. On paper it's ~5–10% faster; in a closet running Docker you will not feel it. Buy it if the price is close to N100 boxes, not because the chip is a leap. See our EQ14 vs G3 Plus breakdown.

  • CPU: Intel N150, 4C/4T, 6W TDP
  • RAM: 16GB DDR5 (typical config)
  • Storage: 512GB NVMe
  • Net: 2× 2.5GbE
  • Price: ~$229

Good

  • Newest low-power Intel chip
  • Same idle sipping as N100

Watch out

  • Small premium over N100 for small gains
Best value N150

GMKtec G3 Plus (Intel N150)

The G3 Plus undercuts the EQ14 while offering the same N150 silicon and dual 2.5GbE. Between the two N150 boxes here, this is the one we'd buy — the comparison is closer than the price gap suggests.

  • CPU: Intel N150, 4C/4T, 6W TDP
  • RAM: 16GB DDR5 (typical config)
  • Storage: 512GB NVMe
  • Net: 2× 2.5GbE
  • Price: ~$209

Good

  • Cheapest path to the N150
  • Full 16GB DDR5

Watch out

  • Brand support is forum-and-email tier
Premium build

Minisforum NAB6 (Intel N100)

An N100 in a nicer suit: better chassis, better thermals, Minisforum's more established support. You pay for the badge, not the benchmark — performance is the same N100 as the budget boxes. Worth it if the box lives somewhere visible or you value the warranty.

  • CPU: Intel N100, 4C/4T, 6W TDP
  • RAM: 16GB DDR5 (typical config)
  • Storage: 512GB NVMe
  • Net: 2× 2.5GbE
  • Price: ~$239

Good

  • Solid build quality and cooling
  • More established vendor

Watch out

  • Same chip as boxes $80 cheaper
Power pick

Minisforum UM690S (Ryzen 9 6900HX)

Not a low-power pick — this is the "I outgrew the N100" box. Eight full cores for VMs, game servers, and Plex transcodes the little chips can't touch. Expect roughly 3–4× the idle power draw of the N100 boxes (illustrative — measure your own), so only buy the cores you'll actually use.

  • CPU: Ryzen 9 6900HX, 8C/16T, 45W
  • RAM: 32GB DDR5 (typical config)
  • Storage: 1TB NVMe
  • Net: 2× 2.5GbE
  • Price: ~$429

Good

  • Serious multi-core muscle
  • Handles VMs and heavy Plex duty

Watch out

  • Much higher idle power than N100/N150
  • Overkill for Docker-only setups

How we pick

For a home server, the ranking criteria are simple, in order: idle power draw (it runs 24/7), networking (dual 2.5GbE is the sweet spot), price, then raw CPU speed. A box that idles at 8W costs roughly $10–15 a year to run (illustrative, at typical US residential rates) — a box idling at 30W costs three to four times that, every year, forever.
